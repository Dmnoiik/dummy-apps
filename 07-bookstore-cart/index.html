The Architecture of Silence

The stone walls of the priory do not merely shut out the gale; they gather silence into a substance that can be weighed against the hand. In early November, when the curlews abandon the high saltings, the dusk falls not as darkness, but as an amber suspension between cloud and peat.

Brother Ewan measured each lintel with a thumb-worn hazel rule. To construct a window for contemplation, he told the novice, one does not frame the horizon—one invites only enough sky to balance the weight of the hearth.
